MANAGEMENT'S DISCUSSION -- OPERATING REVIEW

The financial year under review was executed against an order book that the Board considers
adequate for the near term. Revenue is recognised on the percentage-of-completion basis across the
contract portfolio, and the movement in turnover between the comparative periods reflects the stage
of execution reached on the larger packages rather than any change in the commercial terms on
which work is taken.

Margins remained within the range the Board regards as sustainable for this class of work. Input costs
-- principally cement, steel and bitumen -- continued to move with the published indices, and where a
contract carries a price-variation clause the recovery has been billed in the normal course. Where it
does not, the exposure was absorbed within the tendered rates.

Receivables continue to be dominated by government and public-sector clients, whose payment cycles
are longer than those of private counterparties but whose credit risk the Board assesses as low.
Retention money is released progressively on expiry of the defect liability periods, and no material
amount is regarded as doubtful of recovery at the balance-sheet date.

The Company met its statutory obligations in respect of goods and services tax, tax deducted at source
and provident-fund contributions during the year, and holds current registrations for every jurisdiction
in which it operates.

CREDIT NOTES -- EXPLANATORY NOTE

Credit notes arise in the ordinary course, principally on remeasurement of executed quantities
following joint survey, and on deductions agreed with the client at the time of final billing. They are
recorded against the invoice to which they relate, so the turnover reported in these accounts is stated
net of them. The Board draws attention to this because the gross billing figure, which appears in the
order-book disclosures, is necessarily the larger number.
